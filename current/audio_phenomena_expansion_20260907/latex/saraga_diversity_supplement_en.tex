\subsection{Prospective natural-annotation and Human-diversity extension}
The Saraga collections provide a possible external source of Indian art-music
performances with manually annotated rhythmic cycles, tempi, sections and
melodic phrases (Srinivasamurthy et al., 2021,
\url{https://doi.org/10.18061/emr.v16i1.7641}). This checkpoint is a metadata
audit, not a completed audio acquisition or detector experiment. A pinned
repository snapshot contains251 Carnatic and108 Hindustani recording metadata
entries, with249 and108 mixed-audio checksums respectively. There are54
lead-vocal multitrack checksum entries in this snapshot; the authors' format
webpage lists168, an unresolved source-version discrepancy rather than proof
of either number of physically available isolated vocals. Three
Hindustani titles explicitly indicate introductory speech and require exclusion
or review before any music-only sampling contract.

Potential uses include validation of natural rhythmic/section changes and
melodic recurrence, plus a separate Human-source transfer test. Tala cycle
annotations cannot be silently equated with Western bars, and chroma-path
variation is not automatically chord-progression diversity. Assay and detector
test groups must remain performer/concert-disjoint. All currently failed vocal
measurement gates remain unchanged.

All2949 repository blobs were matched to the pinned Git tree, but no physical
audio duration or classifier performance is established. The repository and
Zenodo record retain different noncommercial CC license labels; neither was
silently replaced. Evidence and the complete limitations are in
\path{artifacts/audio_phenomena_expansion_20260907/SARAGA_DIVERSITY_REVIEW_EN.md},
\path{artifacts/audio_phenomena_expansion_20260907/audit/saraga_catalog_v1.json},
and \path{artifacts/audio_phenomena_expansion_20260907/code/audit_saraga_catalog.py}.
