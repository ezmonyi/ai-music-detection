\subsection{A separately reserved Mureka generator cohort}

To address the single-generator limitation of the long-context development
cohort, we acquired 500 Mureka v9 recordings from the uploader-labelled
\texttt{homura23/MUSIC8K} repository at revision
\texttt{05232438ba76a7bc55cbebfdc6d5f4011c980bba}.
This is dataset provenance, not independently verified generation logs.
After excluding 12 feasibility probes, we deterministically ranked the
remaining 650 candidates by a frozen seeded SHA-256 rule and selected the
first 500 without replacement based on model scores or audio quality.

All 500 original MP3 files were downloaded and fully decoded with FFmpeg;
all samples were finite. An independent acquisition auditor reconstructed the
selection and rehashed all original files, totaling 2,411,879,065 bytes. All
files were 44.1-kHz stereo, with decoded durations from 98.39 to 313.19 seconds,
and all supported a genuine 60-second interval without padding. This auditor
checked the full-decode receipts; it did not perform a second full decode.
Metadata and literal/hash overlap screens found no matches under their stated
rules, but do not establish perceptual or semantic independence.

The measurement protocol fixes the native interval to
\(s=\lfloor(N-2646000)/2\rfloor\), of length 2,646,000 frames. The four existing
families use a stereo FLOAT WAV, without additional gain, limiting or DC
removal. F/H/M use the same native interval through their unchanged
native-to-16-kHz mono front end. Before preparation is frozen, remote-copy
hash verification and the analysis decoder's frame/rate/channel agreement
with the acquisition receipt are required for every file. Synthetic tests of
the preparation software are not evidence that this real-data gate has passed.

The new cohort is reserved for measurement and eventual transfer evaluation;
it is not silently appended to the existing 1,604 development rows. Frozen
and audited old-cohort models must be evaluated before adaptation to Mureka.
An AI-only cohort directly estimates sensitivity and false-negative rate,
not specificity, balanced accuracy or AUC. Comparisons against previously
analyzed Human out-of-fold scores must be labelled as historical-reference
comparisons, not untouched external two-class tests. No new-generator
detector performance is reported in this measurement supplement.

\paragraph{A decoder discrepancy caught before preparation.}
The complete remote raw-file and receipt hash audit passed for all 500 items.
The subsequent preparation freeze nevertheless failed safely on one MP3,
item 97136; no measurement contract or crop was published. Its container
reported approximately 217.27 seconds, whereas two full FFmpeg decodes yielded
6,157,487 identical stereo frames (139.626 seconds). Sequential soundfile
reads yielded 6,156,335 frames, while its block iterator returned additional
nonzero repeated-buffer content. Such an iterator count is not proof of real
audio duration and must not be interpreted as a musical recurrence phenomenon.
The other 499 header comparisons matched their acquisition records; this is
not a claim that all their sequential EOF lengths were independently checked.

A diagnostic no-reencoding remux preserved the full FFmpeg-decoded sample
hash and corrected the header, but did not resolve soundfile's remaining
1,152-frame EOF deficit. It was therefore not admitted as a complete repair.
For the original file, the acquisition-defined center interval beginning at
frame 1,755,743 contains all 2,646,000 requested samples. Fresh and fully-scanned
seeks match the sequential interval exactly. Three bounded cross-decoder
windows show no best-alignment offset and maximum sample error below
\(10^{-6}\); these windows do not establish bit-identical complete decodes.
The original source, rejected freeze, diagnostic derivative and observations
remain preserved. A separately reviewed interval-based v2 admission rule was
then implemented without changing v1 or replacing any source.

\paragraph{Uniform interval verification in v2.}
The same rule is applied to all 500 recordings: sequential float64 reads
continue until an empty return; actual EOF must contain the acquisition-defined
center interval; and a fresh seek must return exactly the same 2,646,000 stereo
samples as the sequential crop. Header, acquisition and sequential lengths
are retained separately. Full-stream decoder equivalence is not claimed.
Float64 and float32 crop hashes are frozen before materialization, then checked
again when creating the stereo FLOAT view. There is no gain adjustment,
short-input padding or diagnostic-remux substitution.

The v2 preparation, neural/strict-audit adapter and F/H/M acceptance suites
passed 17, 30 and 7 synthetic CPU tests, respectively. At this checkpoint the
real 500-item sequential verification has passed and the interval contract is
frozen. Exactly one item (97136) has both a header discrepancy and an actual
EOF discrepancy; every other item agrees with its acquisition frame count.
All 500 selected intervals passed the identical finite-sample and float64
seek-versus-sequential equality rule. Materialization of the standardized WAV
views is running; neither feature measurement nor evaluation is complete.
No new-source classifier has been fitted or
scored. The F/H/M acceptance procedure checks every scalar feature against
its authoritative item record and preserves all unavailable values. The
loader's reported total-frame field is interpreted as a header observation,
not substituted for actual EOF evidence.
