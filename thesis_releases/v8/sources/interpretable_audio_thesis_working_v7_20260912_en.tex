\begin{abstract}
This thesis investigates interpretable spectral, dynamics, rhythm and structural
measurements for distinguishing source-labelled AI-generated and human music.
External controls test sensitivity to acoustic phenomena, while grouped,
source-held-out and generator-held-out evaluations test distinct discrimination
targets. On the expanded 4,228-recording Native30 development cohort, the
spectral/dynamics/rhythm/phrase configuration achieves 69.72\% group/source-balanced
accuracy under generator holdout, compared with 66.29\% for all eight evaluated
families. The eight-family result is higher under ordinary grouped evaluation
(78.37\%), illustrating the importance of the evaluation target. A separate
frozen-model Native60 transfer yields 91.70\% mean AI sensitivity for the four
families on 53 length-eligible locked YuE2 recordings, but cannot estimate human
specificity. The study preserves failed attempts, intermediate outputs and
reproducibility evidence. Its findings support measurable label associations,
not an intrinsic or universally reliable acoustic signature of AI authorship.
\end{abstract}

\section{Synthesis and remaining limitations}
The completed experiments show that interpretable measurements can provide
label-associated information without uniquely identifying authorship. The
Native30 results reinforce a central generalization limit: increasing feature
count or training quantity does not guarantee better held-generator decisions.
External measurement validation, source-held-out classification, and faithful
reproduction are separate requirements. No descriptor family should be accepted
as a causal AI signature merely because it improves one fitted classifier.

Native-channel and duration eligibility select particular populations. Source
labels incompletely represent genre, language, culture, instrumentation, editing,
codec, and mastering practice. Pretrained separators and music-understanding
front ends add their own domain sensitivity. Human-produced music may use MIDI,
synthesis, and extensive processing. These detectors should not be used as sole
evidence to penalize a musician or certify a recording.

The completed Native60 transfer adds an AI-only, duration-selected endpoint;
it does not resolve the human false-positive question. The YuE2 extension has
398 eligible development recordings and 100 separately retained locked-test
recordings; two shorter originals are excluded from Native30 rather than padded.
Shared prompt dependencies must remain excluded across training and evaluation.
