\section{Interpretation and generalization boundaries}
\label{sec:discussion}

This discussion synthesizes completed experiments. The unfinished Native30
seven-family and BC comparisons are not used to support any conclusion here;
their audited outcomes must be incorporated before final submission.

\subsection{Interpretability is a constraint on the claim}
A descriptor can be interpretable because its computation and units are
explicit without being a unique explanation of authorship. Spectral ratios
may reflect a generator, a separator, a codec or a mastering decision.
Onset-conditioned amplitude statistics may respond correctly to a controlled
gain intervention yet also vary with instrumentation. The external tests
therefore constrain what can be said about each measurement. They do not
identify the cause of every observed between-class difference.

This distinction explains why failed gates are not discarded when a
classifier benefits from the corresponding exploratory feature. R and P
may contain information associated with the labels without satisfying the
stronger claim that the complete families reliably measure intended rhythm
and phrase-length variation. Likewise, high breath recall cannot rescue
an unusably low event precision. The thesis reports both levels of evidence
instead of letting a favourable classification score override a measurement
failure.

\subsection{Additional information is conditional, not automatically useful}
The Exact10 H increment is supported by record-group uncertainty conditional
on the retained models and observations, while the source-composition
sensitivity range crosses zero. The Equal60 SC comparison gives a different
warning: an independently informative family can reduce the fixed baseline's
held-generator balanced accuracy. Neither outcome is paradoxical. Additional
features can encode source-specific relationships that the linear model
uses successfully within some partitions but unsuccessfully after a shift.

An increment is therefore defined relative to a particular baseline,
population, split, training cap and missingness mode. It is not a permanent
ranking of phenomena. Exhaustive subset tables expose these dependencies;
selecting their maximum after inspection does not create an independently
confirmed optimum. The present closeout does not add a nested-selection
experiment to resolve that selection uncertainty.

\subsection{Ranking, decisions and source-specific harm}
The separation between AUC and balanced accuracy matters operationally.
A model may rank the two classes in an unfamiliar source reasonably well
while the fixed threshold fails. Here ridge scores are unbounded decision
values, not calibrated probabilities, and the threshold is not retuned on
held-source labels. An ordinary-group result is consequently insufficient
evidence for applying the same decision rule to an unknown generator.

False accusations and missed generated recordings also have different
consequences. Human-source specificity and generator sensitivity must remain
visible beside macro averages. The observed DEAM, Saraga and generator-specific
failures rule out treating these experimental scores as sole evidence for
penalizing a musician or certifying an individual recording. Human labels
also permit MIDI programming, editing and synthesis within human production;
the task is not an acoustic test for the absence of technology.

\subsection{Remaining uncertainty without expanding this experiment}
The retained data increase source diversity, but duration and native-channel
eligibility select different populations. Short-window and long-window
results do not form a clean causal test of listening duration. Five AI
sources in Native30, and only two in Equal60, cannot represent every current
or future generator. Dataset names are also incomplete proxies for genre,
language, culture and production practice.

External controls cover specific interventions and codecs rather than all
possible nuisance transformations. Pretrained front ends introduce their
own domain sensitivity, and some public-data overlap with their training
cannot be excluded from provenance alone. File hashes, deterministic replay
and model-equation audits improve reproducibility but do not remove these
scientific limitations. Broader untouched-source confirmation, calibrated
deployment decisions and independent measurement replication remain future
work, not experiments silently added to the current thesis.
