\section{Controlled-Change Validation and Detector Extension}
\label{sec:change-extension}

\subsection{Why a Second External Test Was Required}

The annotation-fidelity benchmark in the preceding section asks whether an
audio estimator reproduces independently annotated dynamics, tempo variation,
or section-length statistics.  The subsequent experimental request poses a
related but different operational question: can the complete waveform
workflow detect a deliberately introduced change on external music, and does
the identically defined output then add information to the existing AI/Human
classifier?  The controlled-change protocol in \outref{O18} was frozen before
audio transformation or detector-cohort extraction.  The original fidelity
results remain unchanged; the present test appends rather than replaces them.

Three paired interventions were created by \coderef{C26}.  Fifty MAESTRO test
recordings contributed an original 30 s piano excerpt, a constant $-6$ dB
negative control, and a smoothly varying $\pm6$ dB version.  Forty-four SALAMI
recordings contributed an original excerpt, a constant 0.8x-speed negative
control, and a version alternating 0.8x and 1.25x speed every four source
seconds.  The same SALAMI recordings also contributed two four-excerpt
mosaics: one with four equal 7.5 s units and one with 3, 5, 8, and 14 s units.
All variants were stereo, 44.1 kHz, and exactly 30 s.  Source recordings were
not modified.

The waveform dynamics extractor measures the robust span, interquartile
range, and adjacent change of onset-centered harmonic attack strength.  Beat
This! \texttt{final0} supplies beat times for IBI CV, normalized local-tempo
total variation, and tempo entropy.  All-In-One \texttt{harmonix-all} supplies
section boundaries, while Beat This! downbeats convert section duration into
bar-count variety.  The family statistic is the equal-weight average of
robustly scaled paired feature differences.  The frozen sensitivity gate
requires probability of superiority at least 0.75 and a 10,000-replicate
paired-bootstrap 95\% lower bound greater than 0.60.  Code and raw outputs are
indexed as \coderef{C27}--\coderef{C31} and \analysisref{A14}.

Dynamics is therefore the only externally sensitivity-qualified family.  Its
span and IQR features had superiority probabilities 0.960 and 0.880,
respectively, whereas the constant-gain composite shifted in the opposite
direction ($P=0.340$).  The rhythm composite did not pass, although tempo
entropy alone was strongly responsive ($P=0.917$, 95\% CI
[0.806, 1.000]); this is a candidate for a new confirmatory corpus, not a
post-hoc admission in the present experiment.  The constant-speed rhythm
composite was materially smaller and negative ($P=0.318$).

The structure failure was primarily an observability failure.  Only one of 44
paired mosaics yielded a complete paired set of detector-facing section/bar
statistics.  All-In-One commonly represented a 30 s mosaic with one long
same-label segment plus edge fragments, so the four intended lengths were not
available to the summary.  Missing pair contributions were neutral in the
predeclared composite; they did not create a favorable result.  The failed
threshold and low coverage are retained explicitly rather than repaired using
AI/Human labels.

\subsection{Application to the Frozen 2,000-Track Cohort}

The exact workflows were next applied to 500 Human FMA, 500 Suno, 500
HeartMuLa, and 500 ACE-Step tracks with the existing 400/100
development/locked split.  Beat This! produced 1,998 files; two Human tracks
had no output and 14 further Human files were empty.  All-In-One produced
2,000 JSON files.  Every learned inference process ran on RTX 5090 GPUs;
deterministic aggregation ran on CPU.  A symlink-only cohort manifest avoided
rewriting any standardized audio.

The dynamics signal needs one important alignment with the motivating
MIDI-controlled-instrument hypothesis.  The external MAESTRO signal contains
no vocal.  Before inspecting any detector result, the detector-side signal was
therefore frozen as the sample-wise sum of Demucs \texttt{bass},
\texttt{drums}, and \texttt{other}, excluding \texttt{vocals}.  All 2,000
tracks had complete non-vocal stems and dynamics measurements.  This prevents
the earlier vocal high-frequency cues from simply being counted again as an
instrument-dynamics result, although Demucs domain response remains a possible
confound.

A fixed ridge linear classifier ($\alpha=10$) used development-only medians,
standardization, and missingness indicators.  The same locked examples were
used for every paired AUC comparison.  The directly recomputed 24-feature
bias-corrected vocal spectral representation is the baseline.  The only
sensitivity-qualified extension is \texttt{spectral+dynamics}; rhythm,
structure, and \texttt{all-new} results are reported as exploratory because
their external family gates failed.  Evaluation code, per-track scores, and
bootstrap tables are \coderef{C32} and \analysisref{A15}.

The qualified result is positive but modest in the pooled evaluation:
spectral+dynamics increases AUC from 0.8754 to 0.8919, a paired difference of
0.0165 (95\% CI [0.0059, 0.0272]).  More importantly, the increment is positive
in every generator-held-out transfer: 0.0472 for Suno, 0.0179 for HeartMuLa,
and 0.0641 for ACE-Step, with all three intervals above zero.  This pattern is
consistent with additional cross-generator information rather than only a
single-generator fingerprint.  Dynamics alone is also informative, reaching
pooled AUC 0.8156 and held-out AUC 0.8528 for ACE-Step.  Within HeartMuLa and
ACE-Step increments are small because the spectral baselines are already near
their within-domain ceilings.

The exploratory union reaches pooled AUC 0.9139 and improves every held-out
scenario, but it is not an admitted detector layer: rhythm and structure failed
the external family gate.  Structure alone is weak (pooled AUC 0.5663), and its
pooled spectral increment is 0.0049 with a 95\% interval crossing zero.  Its
complete four-feature coverage is also source-dependent: only 7\% of locked
Human, 12\% of locked Suno, 7\% of locked HeartMuLa, and 4\% of locked ACE-Step
tracks.  The classifier can therefore learn availability artifacts through
the missingness indicators.  Rhythm coverage is much higher (90\%--100\% on
locked domains), and its exploratory pooled spectral increment is 0.0175, but
the failed external family gate prevents a confirmatory authorship claim.

\subsection{Interpretation for the Applied Filter}

The deployment recommendation changes in one narrow way.  Non-vocal dynamics
may be enabled as a separately reported, low-weight extension to the vocal
spectral layer because it passes an external change-sensitivity gate and adds
locked cross-generator information.  The system should expose its three
contributing measurements and a valid-stem flag, not present them as direct
evidence of MIDI authorship.  Rhythm entropy is promising but remains a
candidate feature pending a new independently frozen rhythm benchmark.
Section/bar-length variety remains disabled for decision making on 30 s
windows because the estimator did not observe the controlled changes reliably
and its coverage is strongly domain-dependent.

These results still do not establish a universal AI-music detector.  The
external controlled transformations validate sensitivity to the specified
interventions, not construct specificity; a compressor, human quantization,
genre, arrangement, mastering, or separator behavior can produce similar
values.  The existing independent AI/Human external test was not reopened for
feature selection.  The new extension should therefore be frozen and tested
there, or preferably on a new generator-disjoint and production-matched test,
only after this thesis-stage analysis is finalized.
