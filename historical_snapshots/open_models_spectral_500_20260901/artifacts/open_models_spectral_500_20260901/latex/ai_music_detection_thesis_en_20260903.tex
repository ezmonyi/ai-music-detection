\begin{abstract}
This report extends an existing Human-versus-Suno spectral study to HeartMuLa and ACE-Step 1.5 and adds separately frozen validation of dynamics, rhythm, and structure features. The spectral workflow covers a 500-item conditional manifest, RTX 5090 inference, unified audio standardization, Demucs vocal separation, separator-response correction, and within-domain, cross-generator, prompt-paired, and duration-sensitivity analyses. Vocal stems of all three AI generators deviate from Human music in the 10--16 kHz region, while the 16--20 kHz direction depends on the generator. The highest within-domain locked-test ROC-AUC values are 0.922 for Suno, 1.000 for HeartMuLa, and 0.988 for ACE-Step, but cross-generator transfer remains asymmetric. A MIDI-grounded Groove MIDI Dataset probe rejects Music Flamingo as a quantitative dynamics/rhythm extractor. A subsequent independent fidelity benchmark shows that base beat/downbeat localization and event-level piano dynamics are measurable, but its detector-facing summaries fail external fidelity thresholds. A separately frozen controlled-change benchmark then tests the operational end-to-end question requested for all three families. Non-vocal dynamics passes its external sensitivity gate and increases pooled spectral AUC from 0.8754 to 0.8919, with positive increments for every held-out generator. Rhythm and section/bar-length family gates fail; their AI/Human ablations remain exploratory even when their union improves cohort AUC. Finally, a prompt-paired external test set is frozen with 500 tracks from each of ten non-Suno, non-HeartMuLa, non-ACE-Step generators and 500 shared Human controls; detector evaluation on this set is intentionally pending. Stable file references are supplied for every method, log, output, result table, and figure.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\fcolorbox{black!25}{codegray}{%
\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\textbf{Scope of the conclusion.} This study establishes separable spectral evidence and a modest incremental non-vocal dynamics signal within the frozen experimental domains; it does not establish a universal AI-music detector. The Human FMA controls are not track-matched to the Muse-conditioned generated songs. Genre, vocal activity, language, mastering, and source provenance may contribute to discrimination. The GMD and GTZAN/MAESTRO/SALAMI experiments test measurement capability or change sensitivity; the subsequent cohort ablation measures association, not causal authorship.}}

\section{Research Questions and Experimental Scope}
\label{sec:scope}

The study begins with a subjective but operational listening observation: vocals, cymbals, sibilants, and other high-frequency transients often give AI-generated music a different sense of ``air,'' grain, bandwidth edge, or over-smoothing from human recordings. This observation is separated into four research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Can this perceptual cue be captured by interpretable measurements of spectral tilt, band-energy ratios, spectral texture, modulation, and pitch periodicity?
  \item After reducing arrangement-level interference and analyzing only Demucs-separated vocals, do Human--AI differences remain?
  \item Do the differences disappear after applying an externally frozen estimate of the average Demucs frequency-response bias?
  \item When transferred across Suno, HeartMuLa, and ACE-Step, do the measurements describe a general AI cue or a fingerprint of a particular generator or codec?
  \item After external controlled-change validation, do non-vocal dynamics, rhythm change, or section/bar-length variety add locked and generator-held-out information beyond the vocal spectral baseline?
\end{enumerate}

HeartMuLa is an open music-foundation-model family containing HeartCodec and autoregressive song-generation models \citep{heartmula2026,heartlib2026}. ACE-Step 1.5 combines language-model planning with diffusion-based generation \citep{acestep2026,acestepcode2026}. This extension generates 500 items from each system and compares them with the existing 500 Suno and 500 Human items. The frozen experimental protocol and research notes are indexed as \outref{O10} and \outref{O11}.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Data Domains and Frozen Splits}

The Human controls come from the FMA domain \citep{fma2017}. The AI domains are Suno, HeartMuLa, and ACE-Step. HeartMuLa and ACE-Step use text metadata from the open Muse dataset \citep{muse2026,musedataset2026}. The accompanying Muse Suno V5 audio was \textbf{not downloaded, decoded, or analyzed}; consequently, no Muse waveform enters this experiment.

The frozen prompt manifest was constructed by \coderef{C01}. Its SHA-256 digest is:

\subsection{Inference Parameters and Compute Constraints}

Model inference and Demucs separation were performed only on the RTX 5090 GPUs of host \texttt{5090-4}; UVR was not run locally. Before allocating the host, GPU memory, utilization, compute processes, host load, and disk headroom were checked independently on 5090-2, 5090-4, and 5090-5. At that time, all eight GPUs on 5090-4 showed approximately 2 MiB allocated memory and 0\% utilization, so that host was selected as the isolated experimental machine. The environment and selection evidence are preserved in \outref{O06}.

HeartMuLa was invoked through \coderef{C03} with a target duration of 30,000 ms, top-k 50, temperature 1.0, and CFG 1.5. MuLa used bf16, HeartCodec used fp32, and the model and codec remained resident on separate GPUs. ACE-Step was invoked through \coderef{C02} using \texttt{acestep-v15-turbo}, 30 s, eight diffusion steps, and a fixed seed. Thinking/chain-of-thought rewriting and built-in peak normalization were disabled, while the release implementation's DCW setting \texttt{double/0.05/0.02/Haar} was preserved.

\subsection{Unified Audio Protocol}

Raw outputs from both new generators were standardized by \coderef{C05} to:

\begin{itemize}
  \item exactly 30 s;
  \item 44.1 kHz sampling rate;
  \item stereo;
  \item 1,323,000 frames per channel;
  \item PCM-16 FLAC; and
  \item no loudness normalization, equalization, or full-track peak normalization.
\end{itemize}

Standardization consisted only of SoundFile decoding, SciPy \texttt{resample\_poly} polyphase resampling, channel normalization, trimming or zero-padding, and PCM encoding. The few samples that exceeded the valid amplitude range after resampling were clipped individually and counted. \coderef{C06} verified sampling rate, duration, channels, finite values, and file counts. When an atomic rename had succeeded but a controlled interruption prevented a state append, \coderef{C04} reconstructed only the missing state entry and did not rewrite the audio.

\subsection{Demucs Vocal Separation}

Separation used \texttt{htdemucs} from Hybrid Transformer Demucs \citep{demucs2021}, a hybrid waveform-and-spectrogram source-separation model. MUSDB18 is a standard associated separation benchmark \citep{musdb18}. The batch wrapper is \coderef{C07}, with the following frozen parameters:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption={Frozen Demucs vocal-only parameters.}]
htdemucs --two-stems vocals --float32 \
  --shifts 0 --overlap 0.25 --segment 7 --jobs 1
\end{lstlisting}

The runtime used Demucs 4.0.1 and PyTorch/Torchaudio 2.7.1+cu128. The read-only environment freeze is in \outref{O06}. After a smoke test, formal separation ran only on GPU 3. Each input produced \texttt{vocals.wav} and \texttt{no\_vocals.wav}; both were 44.1 kHz, stereo, 30 s, float32 WAV.

\section{Spectral Measurements and Statistical Methods}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{STFT and Spectral Shape}

All metrics were computed on mono vocal stems. Stereo stems were averaged and DC removed. Let $x[m]$ denote the waveform, with a Hann window of length $N=4096$ and hop $H=1024$:
\begin{equation}
X(n,k)=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}x[nH+m]w[m]\exp\!\left(-j\frac{2\pi km}{N}\right).
\label{eq:stft}
\end{equation}
Power was defined as $P(n,k)=|X(n,k)|^2+\epsilon$. The time-frame rate for a 30 s recording was approximately $44100/1024\approx43.1$ Hz. Average-difference heatmaps used 128 logarithmic bands from 300 Hz to 20 kHz and were generated by \coderef{C12}.

The absolute representation preserved overall level. The frame-normalized representation subtracted the mean over the 300 Hz--20 kHz bands from each frame:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{shape}}(n,b)=S(n,b)-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{b'\in\mathcal{B}}S(n,b').
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
This operation attenuated global level differences and emphasized the relative distribution of energy along the frequency axis. The population-average difference between AI and Human was defined as
\begin{equation}
\Delta(n,b)=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{AI}}}\sum_i S_i^{\mathrm{AI}}(n,b)
-\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{H}}}\sum_j S_j^{\mathrm{H}}(n,b).
\label{eq:average-diff}
\end{equation}
Songs were not waveform-aligned. Therefore, the horizontal axis describes a shared position in the 30 s structural window and must not be interpreted as sample-wise cancellation.

\subsection{Frozen Demucs Frequency-Response Correction}

Let $b(k)$ dB denote the average Demucs frequency bias estimated on external MUSDB oracle vocals. It was frozen before the current AI/Human labels were examined and applied identically to every vocal stem:
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathrm{corr}}(n,k)=X(n,k)10^{-b(k)/20}.
\label{eq:bias-correction}
\end{equation}
Phase was preserved, followed by ISTFT overlap-add and DC removal. The implementation in \coderef{C11} first performed an STFT/ISTFT identity test with zero bias, requiring relative error no greater than $10^{-8}$. This correction removes only the shared average separator response; it cannot remove class-dependent separation error.

\subsection{Interpretable Scalar Metrics}

Metric computation is centralized in \coderef{C09} and restricted to active frames. The activity threshold for each song was the greater of the 30th percentile of vocal-frame RMS and 35 dB below that song's maximum RMS. If fewer than 16 frames remained, the 16 highest-RMS frames were retained. The metrics were organized into four groups.

The high-frequency ratio was $10\log_{10}(E_{5-16\mathrm{k}}/E_{1-5\mathrm{k}})$, and the air ratio compared 12--20 kHz with 1--5 kHz. Spectral tilt was the linear slope of mean-band dB against $\log_2 f$. The sibilance-burst threshold was the within-song median sibilance ratio plus 1.5 times its median absolute deviation; burst count was divided by active duration.

\subsection{Frequency-Band Vectors and Classification}

In addition to the 22 scalar metrics, \coderef{C10} constructed a vector for each frequency representation. Each range was divided into 48 geometrically spaced bands. The active-frame mean and standard deviation in dB were recorded for every band, after which the mean components were centered. The ablations were full 0.3--20 kHz, only 5--10 kHz, without 5--10 kHz, only 5--16 kHz, and low-mid 0.3--5 kHz.

The classifier was a standardized ridge linear score with $\alpha=10$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{w}}=(\mathbf{Z}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{Z}+\alpha\mathbf{I})^{-1}
\mathbf{Z}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf{y}'-\bar{y}'),\qquad y'=2y-1.
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
Development data were used only for fitting; locked-test data were reserved for final evaluation. Reported quantities were ROC-AUC, balanced accuracy at threshold 0, and class-stratified bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals. Within-domain analyses used 2,000 bootstrap samples by default; cross-generator and duration-sensitivity analyses used 1,000. Univariate comparisons used the Mann--Whitney U test, prompt-paired comparisons used the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and both applied Benjamini--Hochberg multiple-testing correction.

\section{Integrated Conclusions and Applied-Filter Design}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Conclusions Supported by the Current Evidence}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The 10--16 kHz region is the most consistent anomalous band across the three current AI domains.} Each generator has a positive frame-normalized mean relative to Human, though the magnitude differs.
  \item \textbf{Anomalies in 16--20 kHz must be detected in both directions.} HeartMuLa has a sharp cutoff above approximately 16.5 kHz, whereas ACE-Step has an upper-band excess. A rule that equates more high-frequency energy with AI would miss HeartMuLa.
  \item \textbf{Static spectral shape and dynamic texture should be combined.} HF tilt, band ratios, crest, modulation, sibilance bursts, HNR, and pitch confidence describe complementary phenomena.
  \item \textbf{The frozen average Demucs correction does not change the main conclusions.} This weakens the explanation that a shared average separator response creates the result, but it does not exclude class-dependent error.
  \item \textbf{Within-domain AUC substantially overstates universality.} Cross-generator score reversal demonstrates sensitivity to codec, bandwidth, and generation-pipeline fingerprints.
  \item \textbf{Music Flamingo is not a validated dynamics or rhythm feature.} The MIDI-grounded capability probe produced 0/22 dynamics accuracy, 3/10 rhythm accuracy, and zero physical-choice order consistency.
  \item \textbf{External fidelity and controlled-change sensitivity answer different questions.} The original detector-facing summaries fail the strict annotation-fidelity gate, but the subsequently frozen end-to-end manipulation test qualifies the non-vocal dynamics family. Its addition raises pooled spectral AUC from 0.8754 to 0.8919 and produces positive, non-zero-crossing increments for all three held-out generators. Rhythm and section/bar-length family gates still fail, so their favorable cohort ablations remain exploratory.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Recommended Layered Applied Filter}

Given the current evidence, the thesis prototype should be organized into four layers instead of treating a within-domain ridge classifier as the final detector:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cross-model layer:} compute the 0.3--20 kHz frame-normalized shape, 5--16 kHz tilt and ratio, 10--16 kHz excess, sibilance bursts, HNR, and pitch confidence on the Demucs vocal stem. Train on multiple generators in development, then evaluate on locked and unseen generators.
  \item \textbf{Fingerprint layer:} explicitly detect bandwidth cutoffs, 16--20 kHz excess, comb-like periodicity, HF crest, and HF modulation. This layer should answer which known generator a sample resembles, rather than directly equating resemblance with AI status.
  \item \textbf{Qualified non-vocal dynamics layer:} sum the Demucs bass, drums, and other stems; report attack-level span, IQR, and adjacent change with a validity flag. Use this as a low-weight extension because its pooled increment is modest and separator/production confounding remains possible.
  \item \textbf{Deferred rhythm and structure layer:} retain tempo entropy as a candidate for a new confirmatory corpus, but do not activate the current rhythm family. Disable All-In-One section/bar-length variety for 30 s decisions because the external gate and complete-feature coverage fail. Music Flamingo judgments remain disabled.
\end{enumerate}

The final system should report a continuous score, the principal contributing bands, a vocal-activity flag, and a domain/out-of-distribution warning. A hard real-world decision threshold is not justified until a matched Human cohort and unseen-generator validation are available.

\subsection{Required Limitations and Next Experiments}

The 500 Muse prompts are strongly biased toward Pop (319), Ballad (292), Female Vocal (286), Piano (263), and C-pop (233). The 500 Human FMA items are dominated by Rock (163), Electronic (115), Experimental (45), and Hip-Hop (42), with only 21 Pop items. Current AUC can therefore contain genuine generator fingerprints together with genre, language, vocal-arrangement, mastering, and provenance differences. The next priorities are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item construct a Human cohort matched as closely as possible in style, language, vocal activity, and mastering;
  \item evaluate the frozen six-generator pristine AIME cohort first, without changing the current representation or threshold, and report all six per-generator results rather than only a pooled score;
  \item test robustness under MP3/AAC compression, resampling, equalization, loudness normalization, and mastering chains;
  \item repeat the key comparisons using oracle vocals or multiple separators to estimate class-dependent separation error; and
  \item align the spectral measurements with blinded listening labels to test whether they actually explain the perceived high-frequency cue;
  \item replicate the language-model capability test on a new, exactly A/B-balanced corpus with a forced-choice prompt frozen in advance;
  \item repeat the qualified non-vocal dynamics result with oracle or alternative stems, compression/mastering perturbations, and a new production-matched generator-disjoint test;
  \item freeze tempo entropy alone on a new external corpus before considering its favorable exploratory detector increment; and
  \item replace or lengthen the section estimator before re-testing section/bar variety, since 30 s complete-feature coverage is only 4--12\% in most locked domains.
\end{enumerate}
