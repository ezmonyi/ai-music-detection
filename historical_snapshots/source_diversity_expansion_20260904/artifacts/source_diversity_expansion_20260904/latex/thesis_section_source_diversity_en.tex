\section{Catalogue-First Human Source-Diversity Expansion}
\label{sec:human-source-diversity}

\subsection{Rationale and Freeze Boundary}

The four-family ablation showed that adding examples from an already represented
generator changed transfer much less than adding generator families.  The same
principle applies to Human music: 500 rows from one catalogue are not equivalent to
500 independent recording domains.  The present extension therefore removes any
requirement that every Human dataset contribute 500 tracks.  It instead retains
small catalogues when they add a distinct performance, instrumentation, production,
or cultural domain, and assigns equal total weight to each eligible source within
the Human class.

The v2 item list was selected from metadata before audio decoding, Demucs, feature
extraction, detector scoring, or threshold adjustment.  The earlier v1 list was
superseded at that point and is preserved only as an audit trail.  The v2 manifest,
protocol, dataset audit, and selection code are indexed as \outref{O30},
\outref{O31}, and \coderef{C43}.  Its SHA-256 digest is

Multiple clips, captions, stems, or extensions from the same underlying work are one
statistical group.  For the Hindustani stress set, exactly two source videos were
selected for each of 50 raags and only one chunk from each video was retained.  For
MagnaTagATune, only one excerpt from an underlying track was retained and artist
counts were capped.  The 50 MAESTRO recordings used in the external dynamics
benchmark were excluded.  MedleyDB is development-only because a separate single
track had already informed the separator-bias study.  MusicNet and DEAM remain
entirely locked.

The already materialized AIME MTG-Jamendo controls remain useful project data, but a
second MTG-Jamendo or Song Describer mirror is not counted as another independent
Human source.  A 16 kHz MTG-Jamendo mirror was also rejected for the spectral family
because its 8 kHz Nyquist limit removes the high-frequency region under study.
Saraga is a strong future candidate---108 Hindustani and 249 Carnatic recordings,
including rhythm and structure annotations---but it was not frozen because the
official audio access is token-based and no equivalently documented HF audio mirror
was found.

\subsection{Materialized Pilot and Integrity Checks}

The smallest two complementary sources were materialized first on the remote host:
all 44 URMP ensemble mixes and 100 Hindustani source-video groups.  The process used
only download, decoding, deterministic interior cropping, and conversion to 10 s,
44.1 kHz, stereo PCM-16 FLAC; no Demucs, UVR, music-understanding model, or detector
feature was run.  All 144 native files were 48 kHz and therefore genuinely eligible
through 20 kHz before resampling.  The standardized files total 103,882,717 bytes.
Every output SHA-256 was recomputed successfully, all 144 manifest IDs were present,
and no temporary raw file remained.  The materializer and validators are indexed as
\coderef{C44} and \coderef{C45}; the local metadata/result ledger is \analysisref{A22}
and the complete remote location is \outref{O32}.

\subsection{Consequence for Detector Evaluation}

This extension is a data-design and input-integrity result, not a new detector-score
result.  None of the new Human tracks has yet changed the reported S, D, R, or P
accuracy.  The next development comparison must balance AI and Human class weight,
give each Human catalogue equal total weight, and evaluate leave-one-Human-catalogue-
out alongside leave-one-generator-out transfer.  MusicNet and DEAM must then be
opened only once for the final frozen S/D/R/P pipeline.  Track-macro and source-macro
metrics must both be reported: the former measures item performance, while the latter
prevents a large catalogue from hiding failure on a small but distinct domain.
