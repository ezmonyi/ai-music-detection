\label{sec:expanded-materialized}

\subsection{Aim and separation of evidence}

The next experiment addresses two different limitations of the preceding
development result: incomplete materialization of the frozen catalogue expansion,
and a restricted distribution of Human reference music. It also separates three
objects that must not be conflated. The early three-generator average-difference
plots describe their original cohort. The later per-recording spectral table
contains 7,100 development inputs, not only the first 1,500 AI recordings. Finally,
the MUSDB-based separator-response correction is an external calibration object;
it is intentionally not re-estimated using AI-versus-Human labels.

The four families are denoted S (vocal spectral measurements), D (non-vocal
attack-level variation), R (beat-interval and local-tempo variation), and P
(estimated section-length and downbeat-span variation). This study tests their
classification contribution. It does not convert a source-classification result
into proof that these proxies measure MIDI velocity, rhythmic creativity, or
human musical intention. The preceding controlled-change evidence qualifies D
only for amplitude-change sensitivity; R and P remain exploratory because their
external measurement gates failed.

\subsection{Frozen acquisition and recording identities}

The selected expansion is the unchanged 2,641-item v2 manifest, with SHA-256
\hashvalue{d9695922b46bae77b8d9572f450e38e1e637827b9039239283dbbc19a3333d26}.
It targets 2,091 Human recordings from nine catalogues, 500 provisionally labelled

Among the new Human recordings, 1,261 belong to development, 630 MusicNet/DEAM

The completed union is 10,141 selected recordings: 3,091 Human and 7,050

the small DiffRhythm pilot; it is not a count of approved new AI training examples.

Native files, pinned source archives or parquet containers, deterministic

has source identity, revision, role, crop offsets, native sample rate/duration,

Retrieval failures remain explicit and do not trigger replacement sampling.

HeartMuLa and ACE-Step are linked by their Muse source-song identity. Reliable

that every cross-catalogue re-recording, cover, or transcoded duplicate is known.
A development group overlapping a locked group is excluded from fitting.

\subsection{Duration and bandwidth controls}

The broad experiment uses exact 10-second, 44.1-kHz, stereo PCM-16 FLAC inputs.
A separate 30-second experiment includes only recordings with at least 30 seconds
of actual native and decoded audio. Forty-two early HeartMuLa outputs are too
short for this experiment. Cached AIME parquet contains sufficient native audio
for 500 Udio and 500 MTG-Jamendo recordings; their restored 30-second views contain
the previous 10-second anchors. The other short AIME outputs are not padded to
manufacture long-window eligibility. Thus the two duration experiments have
different cohorts, and their aggregate scores are not a matched duration effect.

Resampling to 44.1 kHz standardizes a representation but cannot reconstruct absent
native bandwidth. Three spectral representations are therefore kept separate:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A primary partial-bandwidth control, S8, uses 15 statistics with its highest
analysis bin below 7.5 kHz and requires a native sample rate of at least 16 kHz.
Its high band is 5--7.5 kHz, its body reference is 1--5 kHz, and its lower
sibilance reference is 0.3--5 kHz. It omits the original 12--20-kHz air ratio.
\item A full-band sensitivity analysis retains the original 16 S statistics only
for recordings with native sample rate at least 40 kHz.
\item A historical-reference analysis retains S16 across all rates. Its results
are explicitly bandwidth-uncontrolled and cannot select the primary pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

S8 is not a low-pass-before-Demucs control: the separator still processes the
original standardized waveform. Generator, codec, mastering, and separator
interactions may remain below 7.5 kHz. The native-rate gate likewise establishes
Nyquist eligibility, not proof of real musical energy throughout the band.

\subsection{Frozen estimator and classifier details}

All neural inference runs on RTX 5090 hardware. All-In-One 3.1.0 with the
\texttt{harmonix-all} checkpoint supplies structure estimates and retained
\texttt{htdemucs} bass, drums, other, and vocal stems. Beat This! uses the
\texttt{final0} checkpoint, half precision, and no DBN post-processing.
Previously computed features are reused only for matching input views and
estimator definitions; input and output hashes make this reuse auditable.
The established Demucs call uses 0.25 overlap, float32 separation, and one
unseeded random temporal shift. Its configured general seed is not applied by the
inference entry point. This recipe is not changed midway through the experiment.
Exact realized stems are preserved by hashes; feature and classifier reproduction
from them is deterministic, whereas bitwise regeneration of the separator output
is not claimed. The group-block bootstrap intervals exclude separator-run variability.
Source-disjoint and locked evaluation refer to fitting the detector in this study;
they do not prove exclusion from the pretraining data of the separator, beat
tracker, or structure model. That separate training-data overlap has not been
exhaustively audited.

The vocal-activity gate is diagnostic only: vocal-to-mixture RMS at least
$-18$ dB, vocal RMS at least $-60$ dBFS, and at least 16 selected frames. It does
not remove instrumental Human recordings from the main cohort and is not supplied
as a classifier feature. Natural unobservability produces missing feature values,
not zero variation; processing failures have separate statuses.

Each classifier is a source/group-weighted ridge least-squares score with penalty
10 and an unpenalized intercept. Training-only medians impute missing values;
missingness indicators are appended, and weighted training means and scales
standardize the resulting columns. Total Human and AI weight is equal; sources
are equal within each class, groups equal within each source, and rows equal
within each group. Weights are rescaled to sum to the training-row count
$N_{\mathrm{train}}$, so the objective is
$\sum_i w_i(y_i-\beta_0-\mathbf{z}_i^\top\boldsymbol{\beta})^2
+10\lVert\boldsymbol{\beta}\rVert_2^2$.
The decision threshold is fixed at 0.5. The unconstrained score is not a
calibrated posterior probability.

The quantity curves characterize this frozen training recipe. Because the loss
is summed with total weight $N_{\mathrm{train}}$, the relative strength of the
fixed penalty changes with training size. Expanding the reference catalogues also
changes the source-balanced training distribution. These comparisons measure the
operational effect of the expanded recipe; they do not isolate a causal effect
of diversity alone at constant sample count and effective regularization.

\subsection{Quantity, combination, and transfer ablations}

All 15 nonempty combinations of S/D/R/P are evaluated at 25, 50, 100, 200, and all
available independent training groups per source. Each cap uses the smaller of
the target and the available source-group count; small catalogues are retained.
Shared AIME/Muse conditions are ordered using a source-independent group hash,
so eligible quantity subsets align across related sources. Actual training rows
and group counts accompany the nominal cap. Prior-only and
expanded training use identical expanded evaluation folds, allowing paired
assessment of the new Human catalogue contribution.

Ordinary five-fold group-disjoint development results are descriptive. Model
selection uses separate leave-one-Human-source-out and leave-one-generator-out
evaluations, each combined with five group-disjoint test folds. A held source is
entirely absent from training, and all members of each evaluated group are absent
as well. For a shared AIME condition, this prevents cross-generator condition
leakage without incorrectly discarding every other AIME training condition.
Metrics are averaged across opposite-source pairs and then across held sources.
The named source/model variant is the primary generator unit. An additional
all-data diagnostic holds out related MusicGen sizes, AudioLDM2 variants, and
Stable Audio versions jointly, comparing the frozen leader with S-only without
changing selection. Variant-heldout accuracy is not presented as evidence of
unseen architecture-family transfer.

The primary exploratory leader is selected at the all-data amount by
\[
J=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{macroAUC}_{\text{held-out Human source}}
 +\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{macroAUC}_{\text{held-out AI generator}}.
\]
Scores within $10^{-12}$ are treated as tied; ties favor fewer feature families,
then the combination name fixes any remaining tie. A wholly unavailable family remains in the
reported ablation but cannot qualify a combination as the selected leader.
All candidates use the same metadata-qualified recordings, avoiding artificially
easy complete-case cohorts for combinations with poor observability.
Missingness-only and median-only/no-indicator checks for the selected leader and
S-only are prespecified diagnostics, not additional selection criteria.

Candidate definitions and fitted models are durably frozen before scoring the
new locked catalogues. New Human specificity is reported separately from pairing
those catalogues with the previously used legacy AI test set. DiffRhythm results
remain a ten-condition exploratory pilot. AudioX receives score distributions,
not accuracy or sensitivity claims based on unverified labels. Paired uncertainty
uses 2,000 group-block bootstrap replicates; repeated file variants are resampled
together, and shared cross-class groups are sampled jointly.
These intervals condition on the frozen fitted models and observed catalogues;
they do not include training or selection variability, nor uncertainty about
unrepresented future sources.

\subsection{Feature completion and measurement availability}

The final feature unions contain exactly 10,141 and 4,497 recording IDs,
respectively, with no unexplained missing rows. S8 and D are computable for all
these inputs. For R, 6,103 ten-second and 4,361 thirty-second inputs meet the
measurement gates. Among the remaining rows, natural R unobservability accounts
for 3,982 and 132, while 56 and four have an explicitly mapped Beat This
serialization failure. The raw unavailable totals, 4,038 and 136, must not all
be reported as natural missingness.

No ten-second input meets the P section-count gate. At thirty seconds, 753
inputs have eligible section-duration measurements, while 3,744 remain naturally
unobservable. All three bar-span measurements are also available for 414 of the
753 duration-eligible rows; the remaining 339 have duration measurements only.
A coincident beat-serialization failure
does not automatically invalidate structure-derived duration measurements: its
dependency marker is retained separately. Thus a classifier contribution from
missingness is not evidence that phrase variety was accurately measured.

Reuse checks on the 7,100-row historical S16 table found a maximum absolute
difference of $1.20\times10^{-12}$ and no changed missing-value pattern.
Stratified twenty-item checks for each duration reproduced the frozen D/R/P
definitions exactly. These checks verify implementation consistency, not musical
construct validity. In particular, the failed external R/P measurement gates
remain failed after data expansion.

\subsection{Completed primary selection results}

The formal orchestration completed all eighteen commands successfully. Both
duration-specific CV selections were frozen and their input hashes rechecked
before any locked scoring. An independent audit confirmed all 150 primary
quantity/combination cells, both AUC and balanced-accuracy matrices, the selection
rules, fixed classifier parameters and the unverified-label boundary.

At ten seconds, S+D is the frozen leader, with Human-source AUC 0.5981,
generator-source AUC 0.6687 and $J=0.6334$. S is the strongest individual family
($J=0.6191$), followed by D (0.5487); R is below chance on the aggregate criterion
(0.4791). P produces a constant, wholly unavailable representation and is not a
qualified candidate. Adding R to S+D reduces the observed $J$ to 0.6272; adding
the unavailable P changes nothing. The corresponding mean fixed-threshold
balanced accuracy of the leader is 0.5971, not 0.6334.

At thirty seconds, S+D+R is selected: Human-source AUC 0.7666,
generator-source AUC 0.7616, $J=0.7641$, and mean balanced accuracy 0.6986.
Individual-family $J$ values are S 0.7186, D 0.6698, R 0.6566 and P 0.5030.
The all-four-family combination attains $J=0.7601$, slightly below the selected three-family
combination. These observed differences do not establish statistical significance:
CV confidence intervals and training/selection uncertainty were not estimated.
The larger long-window score is not a controlled duration comparison, because
the source composition and eligible recordings changed.

\subsection{What the expanded Human reference data changed}

Matched held-out recordings support a much narrower conclusion than a blanket
claim that expansion improves detection. At ten seconds, S+D improves
generator-heldout AUC by 0.0580 but reduces Human-source-heldout AUC by 0.0512;
the net $\Delta J$ is only about 0.0034. S alone has a similarly mixed pattern.
At thirty seconds, the S+D+R changes are positive in both directions
($+0.0310$ Human, $+0.1285$ generator, $\Delta J=+0.0798$). The largest long-window
gain occurs for S alone ($\Delta J=+0.1670$), while combinations without S show
little improvement or small declines. This pattern is consistent with an
overly narrow prior Human reference distribution for spectral measurements.
It is not a causal estimate of diversity alone: training size, source weighting,
and effective regularization also changed.

\subsection{Locked Human specificity and secondary AI comparisons}

The 630 new locked Human recordings are the principal check against false AI
accusations. Ten-second S+D correctly labels 86.36\% of MusicNet and 56.33\%
of DEAM recordings as Human. Thirty-second S+D+R reaches 95.15\% on MusicNet
but only 52.33\% on DEAM. The latter corresponds to a 47.67\% false-positive
rate on that catalogue: this is incompatible with a general-purpose reliable
Human/AI attribution claim, regardless of the strong MusicNet result.

The following tables preserve every source-pair bootstrap condition. The Human
specificity point estimate is repeated because the same frozen Human predictions
are paired with different AI sources; these are not independent Human replications.
At ten seconds, paired specificity increments over S-only are small and their
intervals include zero. At thirty seconds they are 0.0800 for DEAM and 0.0667
for MusicNet, with positive conditional bootstrap intervals. Importantly,
MusicNet and DEAM each use recording-ID groups (330 and 300), not a
composer- or artist-level resampling scheme. Unmodelled creator/composition
dependence can make these intervals too optimistic for those broader targets.
