\section{MIDI-Grounded Validation of a Music-Understanding Model}
\label{sec:music-flamingo-probe}

\subsection{Motivation and Admission Criterion}

The proposed applied filter includes two non-spectral hypotheses: AI-generated
arrangements may exhibit reduced dynamics variation in MIDI-controlled
instruments, and they may exhibit less varied rhythmic development. These
hypotheses can be measured deterministically when aligned MIDI is available.
For waveform-only deployment, a music-understanding model might appear to offer
a convenient semantic measurement. This section therefore tests Music Flamingo
\citep{musicflamingo2025} before allowing any model-derived judgment to enter
the Human--AI detector.

This is a capability-validation experiment, not an AI-versus-Human
classification test. The model feature is admitted only if its lower 95\%
Wilson bound exceeds 0.5, physical-excerpt selection is at least 0.75 consistent
under A/B reversal, exact JSON parse rate is at least 0.95, performance remains
above chance among low-nuisance pairs, and no direction reversal appears across
major styles. Failure leaves the deterministic MIDI/MIR measurement in the
heuristic set and excludes the language-model judgment.

The official Music Flamingo evaluations cover broad captioning, music QA,
instrument, lyric, and reasoning tasks, but do not report dedicated accuracy
for MIDI velocity-variation or bar-to-bar rhythm-variation judgments
\citep{musicflamingohf2026}. MF-Skills was not used for evaluation because its
card describes synthetic/model-assisted annotations and it was used in Music
Flamingo training \citep{mfskills2025}.

\subsection{Open Dataset and Objective Targets}

The open Groove MIDI Dataset (GMD) supplies human-performed drum MIDI, aligned
synthesized audio, velocity and timing, tempo/meter metadata, and an official
test split under CC BY 4.0 \citep{gmd2019}. Only 4/4 \texttt{beat}
performances in the test split were eligible. The official MIDI-only archive
defined every target. Audio was transported through a 16-kHz Hugging Face
mirror at frozen revision
\texttt{a505eb614310d60f6844042709c286b8a4d1988f}; mirror labels were not used
\citep{gmdmirror2026}.

For each pitch $p$ with at least four note onsets, dynamics variation combines
two onset-weighted measurements:
\begin{equation}
  D_p^{(1)} = \frac{\sigma(v_p)}{\mu(v_p)}, \qquad
  D_p^{(2)} = \frac{\operatorname{median}_i |v_{p,i}-v_{p,i-1}|}
  {\operatorname{median}_i v_{p,i}}.
\end{equation}
The primary dynamics score is the equal-weight mean of the within-corpus robust
$z$-scores of the two pitch-conditional summaries. Pitch conditioning prevents
changes in the mixture of kick, snare, cymbal, and tom events from masquerading
as expressive dynamics.

For rhythm, onsets are quantized to a 16th-note grid and mapped to coarse drum
classes. Each of four bars is represented by a binary class-by-grid vector
$b_j$. The rhythm-variation score is the average pairwise Jaccard distance:
\begin{equation}
  R = \frac{1}{\binom{4}{2}}\sum_{i<j}
  \left(1-\frac{|b_i\cap b_j|}{|b_i\cup b_j|}\right).
\end{equation}

The final pool contained 116 eligible four-bar windows from 36 official test
performances. Strict within-performance matching yielded 22 dynamics pairs and
10 rhythm pairs. Dynamics target gaps were 0.531--4.148 (median 0.990), with a
median rhythm-nuisance gap of 0.044 and a median RMS gap of 0.282 dB. Rhythm
target gaps were 0.085--0.255 (median 0.109), with a median dynamics-nuisance
gap of 0.175 and a median RMS gap of 0.244 dB. The shortfall from the intended
32 rhythm pairs was retained rather than weakening matching rules after data
inspection.

\subsection{Pairwise Protocol and 5090 Inference}

Every pair came from the same source performance. Because the released chat
template emits one \texttt{<sound>} placeholder per user turn, excerpt A and
excerpt B were concatenated with one second of silence. Paired durations were
14.913--25.000 s (median 19.824 s), below the 30-s processor window. Each pair
was queried in both orders; the correct physical excerpt is unchanged while its
letter flips.

Only the seed-randomized first ordering of each pair enters primary accuracy and
its Wilson interval, avoiding dependence between an item and its reversal.
Reversed queries measure physical-choice order consistency and positional bias.
\texttt{TIE}, invalid JSON, and runtime failures count as incorrect.

Inference used \path{nvidia/music-flamingo-2601-hf} revision
\MFHash{6b5be086d52f65a1e204cb0faf70bf54e2741ecd}
\citep{musicflamingocheckpoint2026}, bfloat16, greedy decoding, and at most 64
new tokens. It ran only on physical GPU 1 of host \texttt{5090-3}, an NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 5090 exposed as process-local \texttt{cuda:0}. The runtime was
PyTorch 2.11.0+cu128 and Transformers 5.16.1. The 16,534,531,504-byte weights
used LFS SHA-256/OID
\MFHash{4da26452d3978503fb064023a69f7867a1f235a2a5a89d3d9146ebf2e04eb15b}.
Peak allocated memory was 15.62 GiB. The 64 primary queries required 17.53 s
after load, with median generation latency 0.216 s.

\subsection{Post-hoc Forced-Choice Sensitivity}

After the primary TIE collapse was observed, one explicitly post-hoc prompt
removed TIE and required A or B; no audio, order, target, checkpoint, or decode
setting changed. Music Flamingo selected A on all 64 queries. This produced an
apparent dynamics accuracy of 15/22 = 0.682 (95\% CI 0.473--0.836) and rhythm
accuracy of 3/10 = 0.300 (0.108--0.603). Both had a first-position selection
rate of 1.000 and physical-choice order consistency of 0.000. The 0.682 value
is therefore the proportion of randomized first orders whose correct excerpt
happened to occupy position A, not evidence of dynamics perception. This
post-hoc analysis cannot replace the preregistered result.

\subsection{Decision for the Applied Filter}

Music Flamingo is excluded from the current dynamics and rhythm feature set. It
obeyed the JSON output format perfectly, but did not produce a usable,
order-invariant perceptual ranking. The thesis retains the pitch-conditional
velocity statistics and bar-pattern Jaccard distance as deterministic metrics
when MIDI is available. For waveform-only deployment, the appropriate next
features are onset-conditioned energy dispersion, adjacent-onset energy change,
bar-synchronous pattern novelty, onset-density variation, tempogram change, and
self-similarity novelty on percussion or accompaniment stems. These MIR proxies
must be calibrated against aligned MIDI before entering the generator-disjoint
Human--AI ablation.

The result does not show that all music-understanding models are incapable of
these judgments, nor does it measure AI-versus-Human accuracy. GMD is
percussion-only and uses synthesized electronic-drum audio; only ten rhythm
pairs passed strict matching. A future preregistered replication should use a
new corpus, balance A/B labels exactly, freeze a forced-choice prompt in
advance, add calibrated human judgments, and include aligned pitched-instrument
audio/MIDI such as piano before generalizing to bass, guitar, voice, or
orchestral parts.
