\section{External Transfer of the Three Heuristic Families}
\label{sec:external-500-heuristics}

The three previously specified workflows were applied to the frozen external
set in Section~\ref{sec:external-generator-500}. This is the detector-facing
stage of the two-stage validation design: first establish whether a workflow
responds to a controlled change, and only then test whether the same frozen
representation supplies transferable Human-versus-AI information. The first
stage admitted only non-vocal dynamics. Rhythm, structure, and any union that
contains them therefore remain exploratory regardless of the classification
numbers below.

\subsection{Matched 10-Second Protocol and Execution}

The fitted models use only the 1,600 pre-existing development items: 400 Human
FMA recordings and 1,200 Suno, HeartMuLa, and ACE-Step recordings. The 400 old
locked items are excluded. Each training item was deterministically copied as
its first 10.000 seconds so that the training view matches the external set's
44.1 kHz, stereo, PCM-16 FLAC standard. The 5,500 external items were never
used for imputation, scaling, feature selection, model fitting, or threshold
selection. The combined manifest contains 7,100 unique tracks and has SHA-256

The frozen protocol itself has SHA-256

All neural inference ran on the eight RTX 5090 GPUs of host \texttt{5090-5}.
Beat This! \texttt{final0} supplied beats and downbeats with no DBN. All-In-One
\texttt{harmonix-all} supplied section boundaries while retaining its default
FP32 \texttt{htdemucs} bass, drums, other, and vocal stems. An exhaustive audit
found 7,100 structure JSON files, 7,100 spectrogram arrays, and 28,400 non-empty
stem WAV files. Beat This! wrote 7,022 files: 88 were legitimate empty
detections and 78 failed during beat-number serialization with
\texttt{ValueError: Not all downbeats are beats}. Failures remain missing and
are not silently repaired.

For dynamics, the Demucs bass, drums, and other stems were summed and the
10th--90th percentile attack-level span, attack-level IQR, and median adjacent
attack-level change were computed. This is a proxy for non-vocal performance
variation; it is not evidence that a track was authored with MIDI. Rhythm uses
robust IBI CV, normalized tempo total variation, and tempo entropy. Structure
uses section-duration CV/entropy and section-bar CV/off-mode fraction. The
minimum observations remain eight attacks, 16 beats, three sections, and four
downbeats as applicable.

Each family and the all-family union use a ridge score with penalty 10, fitted
once on old development data. The 400 development Human rows are repeated
three times to balance 1,200 AI rows. Missing values use training-only medians
plus explicit missingness indicators. The decision threshold remains 0.5.
Confidence intervals use 2,000 prompt-condition bootstrap replicates.

\subsection{Coverage Is Part of the Result}

Dynamics is complete for all 5,500 external items. Rhythm coverage is
source-dependent: 0.420 for AudioLDM 2 Large, 0.614--0.658 for the three
MusicGen variants, 0.632 for Udio, 0.676 for Stable Audio v1, 0.740 for Stable
Audio v2, 0.842 for Riffusion, and 0.734 for Human MTG-Jamendo. This makes the
analyzer's missingness itself a potential source fingerprint.

Structure coverage is exactly zero for every external source and for the old
10-second development view. The median cleaned All-In-One output contains one
segment, below the frozen minimum of three. The structure weights are therefore
all zero, and its nominal classifier is constant. This is an observability
failure, not evidence that AI and Human section variety are equal.

Dynamics transfers moderately to the three MusicGen variants and Stable Audio
v2, but reverses on AudioLDM 2 Music and especially Riffusion. This is a
generator fingerprint, not a common AI signature. Threshold calibration is
also poor: the all-ten dynamics score has Human specificity 0.780 but AI
sensitivity only 0.249.

The metric directions explain the failure. In development, AI tempo entropy is
lower than Human (median 0.224 versus 0.258), so the frozen rhythm model assigns
it a negative coefficient. In the external set, the direction reverses: AI
median entropy is 0.259 versus Human 0.210, and the raw higher-means-AI AUC is
0.613. Similarly, external Human dynamics span has median 7.33 dB, compared
with 9.57--9.72 dB for MusicGen Small/Medium, 5.14 dB for AudioLDM 2 Music,
and 3.48 dB for Riffusion. One global direction cannot represent these systems.

\subsection{Decision}

This larger test rejects activation of any of the three families as a
standalone general-purpose AI/Human filter. The qualified status of dynamics
means only that the workflow can detect controlled non-vocal dynamic changes;
it does not guarantee a generator-invariant class direction. Rhythm reverses
between development and external domains, and section/bar-length variety is
not measurable in ten seconds with the current minimum-observation rule.
Dynamics and rhythm may still be retained as low-level descriptors in a
known-generator fingerprint or out-of-distribution report. Any new global rule
derived from the present external medians would be post-hoc and must be tested
on another untouched, production-matched collection.
